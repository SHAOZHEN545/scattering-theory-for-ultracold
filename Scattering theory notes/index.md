These notes develop scattering theory from a worked one-dimensional problem to the multichannel description used in ultracold atomic and molecular physics.

## A guide to the notes

The central thread is how a collision becomes a measurable scattering rate. A concrete example introduces the concepts; the later notes build the general framework and connect it to low-energy collisions and resonances. Use the links below to enter at the topic you need.

### From a worked problem to the general framework

- [[Scattering Theory 0 - One Problem Worked Through - The Basic Concepts of Scattering|A worked problem]] — A one-dimensional delta barrier connects the stationary solution to scattering as a process in time.
- [[Scattering Theory 0 Supplement - The General Solution with Both Incidence Directions|Both incidence directions]] — Extends the example to incoming waves from either side.
- [[Scattering Theory 1 - Asymptotic Dynamics and the Lippmann--Schwinger Equation|Asymptotic dynamics]] — Introduces incoming and outgoing states, the scattering operators, and the Lippmann–Schwinger equation.

### From states to observable collisions

- [[Scattering Theory 2 - Channel-Resolved Coordinate and Momentum Representations|Coordinate and momentum representations]] — Expresses multichannel scattering states in the two representations.
- [[Scattering Theory 3 - Channel-Resolved Scattering Amplitudes|Scattering amplitudes]] — Relates the outgoing wave in each channel to the transition operator.
- [[Scattering Theory 4 - Transition Rates, Flux, and Cross Sections|Rates, flux, and cross sections]] — Converts amplitudes into collision probabilities and measurable cross sections.

### Low-energy scattering and resonances

- [[Scattering Theory 5 - Partial Waves, Channel Blocks, and Phase Shifts|Partial waves and phase shifts]] — Organizes scattering by angular momentum and describes each contribution through phase shifts.
- [[Scattering Theory 6 - Low-Energy Scattering, Effective Range, and Scattering Length|Scattering length and effective range]] — Develops the parameters that describe collisions near threshold.
- [[Feshbach Resonance I - Projection Formalism and the Origin of the Resonant Scattering Length|Feshbach resonances]] — Uses open- and closed-channel projection to explain resonant changes in the scattering length.
